# Lens briefs

Use one brief per pass. Fill `{PITCH}` with the idea and `{EVIDENCE}` with the tagged
facts from Step 1 (FACT / CALC / UNVERIFIED), so each lens reasons from evidence, not
vibes. Each lens sees only the pitch and the evidence, never another lens's answer
(except the contrarian, which gets the emerging consensus on purpose).

Every brief works three ways: as a separate pass in one reply, as a subagent prompt,
or pasted into a fresh chat by the user for full independence.

Quick gut-check = customer + skeptic + economist. "Tear it apart" = all six.

---

## Customer: is there real demand?
> Pressure-test this idea from the buyer's side only. Would anyone actually care or pay?
> IDEA: {PITCH}
> EVIDENCE: {EVIDENCE}
> Answer sharply: (1) who is the real buyer, and is the pain strong enough to change
> what they do today? (2) would they trust this, or stick with what they already use?
> (3) would they pay, and does the revenue model match how they actually buy?
> (4) is there a say-do gap: do people act on this, or just like the idea?
> (5) is this recurring, paid demand or a nice-to-have?
> End with one line: REAL PAID DEMAND / WEAK DEMAND / NO DEMAND, and why.

## Skeptic: the kill-shots
> Red-team this idea. Find what ends it, ranked from most to least lethal.
> IDEA: {PITCH}
> EVIDENCE: {EVIDENCE}
> (1) the single most likely thing to kill it; (2) what stops a bigger player copying
> it; (3) a founder-fit risk; (4) a dependency on one channel, platform or supplier;
> (5) a hidden risk not mentioned in the evidence.
> Default to skepticism, but don't invent facts.
> End with one line: FATAL / SURVIVABLE WITH MAJOR CHANGES / MINOR RISKS, naming the
> single most serious flaw.

## Economist: is the juice worth the squeeze?
> Judge this idea on money and time only.
> IDEA: {PITCH}
> EVIDENCE: {EVIDENCE}
> (1) a realistic path to first income from zero, and how long it takes;
> (2) the opportunity cost in the founder's time (months and years, not just cash);
> (3) where the money leaks; (4) is there a better way to make money from the same
> asset or skill? compare rough orders of magnitude; (5) at what scale, if ever, does
> this become a real income?
> Show the math and mark every assumption.
> End with one line: STRONG RETURN / MARGINAL / POOR RETURN, and the binding money
> constraint.

## Contrarian: flip the consensus (runs last)
> The other lenses are converging on: "{EMERGING_VERDICT}". Your job is to argue the
> strongest honest case against that, so the final call isn't confirmation bias.
> IDEA: {PITCH}
> EVIDENCE: {EVIDENCE}
> (1) what version of this becomes real and defensible? (2) why might the main
> objection be the wrong frame? (3) the cheapest wedge to start with; (4) real cases
> where something shaped like this worked (say UNVERIFIED if you can't source them);
> (5) what would have to be true for this to be a big outcome, and is that plausible?
> Persuasive but honest. No hype that won't survive scrutiny.
> End with one line: THE CONSENSUS IS WRONG BECAUSE... / THE CONSENSUS HOLDS, BUT THE
> PIVOT IS..., naming the single strongest path.

## Historian: has this been tried? (optional)
> Has this, or something close, been tried? What usually happens to ideas in this
> category? Name precedents that worked or failed and the specific reason, and mark
> any you can't source as UNVERIFIED.
> End with one line: HISTORY FAVOURS / HISTORY WARNS, with the closest precedent.

## Operator: the Monday-morning path (optional)
> Ignore everything except the binding risk. What is the fastest real path to a first
> proof or first paying customer this week? Concrete steps only, nothing that needs
> money the founder hasn't said they have.
> End with one line: the single first action.

---

## Reading the panel
- Lenses that couldn't see each other and still landed on the same flaw: trust it.
- The contrarian and the economist pointing at the same escape route: that's often
  the pivot.
- Lenses that disagree: report the disagreement and what fact would settle it. Don't
  average it away.
